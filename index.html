Background & Opportunity

EU-India wine trade

The Indian wine market is experiencing unprecedented growth, driven by rising disposable incomes, changing lifestyles, and an increasing preference for premium beverages among urban consumers. This steady rise in domestic consumption, coupled with the rapid expansion of the hospitality sector and the emergence of wine tourism in regions like Maharashtra and Karnataka, has positioned India as one of the most promising emerging markets globally. Backed by active government support for viticulture and local industry development, these shifting market dynamics present substantial opportunities for international, and particularly European, wine producers seeking to establish a foothold in a rapidly expanding consumer base.

On the other hand, Europe stands as the historic heartland and global leader of the wine industry, boasting centuries-old traditions, unmatched quality standards, and iconic wine-producing regions. While European labels enjoy high prestige worldwide, there is also a growing appreciation and presence of quality Indian wines in European markets, creating a fertile ground for bilateral exchange. Beyond direct product trade, this relationship offers immense potential for deep collaboration between the two regions, enabling partnerships in winemaking technology, vineyard investments, and culinary tourism, thereby enriching the wine ecosystems of both Europe and India.

The timing of this initiative is particularly significant in light of the recently concluded India–European Union Free Trade Agreement, a landmark agreement aimed at strengthening economic and trade relations between India and the European Union.

Under this agreement, import tariffs on European wines in India - previously as high as 150% - will be significantly reduced in phases to around 20% for premium wines and about 30% for mid-range wines, making European wines more accessible to the fast-growing Indian market.

This major policy shift opens new opportunities for trade, investment, technology transfer, and joint ventures between European and Indian wine industries.

It will also provide a unique opportunity for Indian state representatives to showcase the potential of their regions, promote viticulture within their states to promote wine industry and explore collaborations with European producers for the development of vineyards and wineries.

Participants: 50–80 small and mid-range European wine producers, Indian buyers, importers, distributors, and industry stakeholders.

Objectives of the Meeting

What this meeting sets
out to achieve

India's wine industry is emerging as a high-potential market, driven by changing consumer preferences, a growing hospitality sector, and increasing support for viticulture from several state governments. At the same time, Europe remains the global leader in winemaking, with centuries of expertise and innovation.

The EU-India Wine Meet 2026, to be held alongside SIAL Paris 2026, will bring together European wine producers, Indian buyers, importers, distributors, policymakers, and representatives from Indian state governments to foster trade, investment, knowledge exchange, and collaboration for the sustainable growth of the wine industry in both regions.

Facilitate Business Discussions

Between European wine producers (France, Germany, Poland, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece and more) and Indian wine importers/Buyers, distributors, and Indian States.

Promote Import Partnerships

For European wines in the Indian market, across red, white, rosé and champagne categories.

Encourage Knowledge Exchange

On branding, distribution, and import processes between both regions' industry participants.

Strengthen Cooperation

Between India and Europe in the wider wine and beverage sector, for the long term.
